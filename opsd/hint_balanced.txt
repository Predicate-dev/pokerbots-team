Strategy notes for this game:
- You are rated on finishing order. Finishing last costs far more than winning earns, and second to last is the
  next worst. So your first job is not to be the first player out, and not to be the smallest stack at the end.
  Only the order counts, never the margin.
- While all six players still have chips, never put your whole stack at risk without QQ, KK, AA or AK. Fold to
  all-ins otherwise, even with good hands. Against a player who moves all-in constantly, add TT, JJ and AQ.
- You still have to win chips: a stack that only folds bleeds away and finishes last at a table where nobody busts.
  Play about three hands in ten. When nobody has raised, open-raise to about three big blinds with any pair, any two
  cards ten or higher, any suited ace, and suited connectors down to 76, and add more hands on the button and in the
  cutoff. Do not limp in.
- Facing a normal raise of up to about four big blinds, continue with pairs, AK down to AT, KQ, KJ suited and suited
  connectors when the price is small. Fold the rest.
- After the flop, if you raised before it, bet about half the pot on most flops against one or two players. Bet
  about two thirds of the pot for value with top pair or better. Give up missed hands when an opponent bets, and do
  not chase draws against large bets.
- Do not fold a strong made hand to a small bet: with two pair or better, call or raise unless the bet is for most
  of your stack and the board makes a better hand very likely. Bet your straights, flushes and sets for value
  instead of checking them down. With a flush draw or an open-ended straight draw, call when the price is small (a
  bet of up to about a third of the pot) and fold it only to large bets.
- As players bust, loosen up. Once two players are out you cannot finish last, so play a normal aggressive game and
  go after chips to move up. When you are comfortably ahead of the players still in near the end, protect the lead.
- Read opponents from their betting. Do not bluff a player who calls everything: bet your good hands for value
  instead. Raise the blinds of a player who folds everything. When a tight player raises big, believe them.
- Keep your reasoning short and decisive, then call submit_action exactly once. Never mention your own cards in
  table talk.
